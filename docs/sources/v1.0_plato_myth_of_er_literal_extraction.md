## Literal findings

Plato describes eight whorls nested within one another. The first is explicitly the outermost, and the spindle shaft passes through the middle of the eighth.

The rim-width order, from broadest to narrowest, is:

```text
whorl 1 > whorl 6 > whorl 4 > whorl 8 > whorl 7 > whorl 5 > whorl 3 > whorl 2
```

This is an ordinal ranking. The passage gives no numerical magnitude or ratio for the rim widths.

The whole spindle turns with one movement while the seven inner circles move gently in the opposite direction. Their speed groups, from fastest to slowest, are:

```text
8 > (7 = 6 = 5) > 4 > 3 > 2
```

The speed order is a separate textual attribute and is not used as a substitute for the rim-width order.

The passage also distinguishes the whorls by appearance and places one Siren on each rim, each sounding one tone; the eight tones combine into a single harmony.

## Negative findings

Within this literal extraction Plato does **not** supply:

- numerical magnitudes for the eight rim widths;
- numerical ratios for those widths;
- Michell's later musical-number width sequence;
- a numerical central-shaft radius;
- a scale factor of 180;
- a New Jerusalem dimensional correspondence.

These absences are part of the historical boundary: later Michell numbers may not be back-projected into Plato.
