\begin{abstract}
John Michell's New Jerusalem drawings juxtapose an Earth--Moon construction,
twelve lunar circles, a twelve-sided enclosure, and seven-pointed stars.
This article examines them as historical visual artefacts through reproducible
Euclidean reconstruction and source-calibrated comparison, while separating
mathematical findings from esoteric interpretation.  A normalized construction
fixes the lunar centres by tangency and circle--square incidence without
fitting source-plate coordinates.  Michell's Figure~12 is consistent with a
nonregular outer wall, although its construction is not uniquely recoverable;
Figure~14 favours the $\{7/2\}$ heptagram topology.  Two Michell sevenfold
procedures yield angles on opposite sides of the exact $360^\circ/7$ step.
The regular heptagon's classical straightedge-and-compass nonconstructibility,
established by Gauss--Wantzel theory, is distinguished from a deliberately
nonclassical single-trisection exercise.  Reproducing Michell's original
diagrams alongside independent reconstructions makes the historical drawings
directly assessable and clarifies the limits of geometrical and historical
inference.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: a historical visual construction as an object of analysis}
John Michell's New Jerusalem diagrams combine a squared Earth circle, twelve surrounding Moon circles, a twelve-sided enclosure and intersecting star polygons in a single visually intricate composition \citep{michell1973city}. These images have often been read alongside Michell's interpretations of cosmology, number and sacred geography. Such interpretations cannot, by themselves, establish the mathematical properties of the drawings. Yet a historically printed diagram can be studied as a visual design even when its author's wider claims are disputed: its circular incidences, symmetries, line connections and departures from regularity are concrete features that can be measured and compared.

The question addressed here is therefore neither whether Michell's metaphysical interpretation is true nor whether its numerical motifs are aesthetically persuasive. It is whether a specific published design can be reconstructed from stated geometric constraints without silently adjusting the result to match its printed appearance, and how much of the resulting agreement can reasonably be attributed to the historical source. That question connects this article to mathematical analyses of artistic and historical geometric designs, rather than to a claim that Michell belonged to an established school of architectural scholarship. In particular, the approach of Costa and Freitas to geometrical artworks distinguishes exact constructions, approximations and computer-based reconstruction in a way that supplies a useful disciplinary precedent \citep{costa2015canon}.

The analysis begins with the visual artefact. Michell's Figure~12, titled ``The New Jerusalem'' in the 1973 Abacus edition (p.~63), presents the twelve-circle geometry and enclosing wall; his Figure~14, ``The 12 gates and 7 hills'' (p.~67), contains a seven-pointed star. Their printed appearance matters: in particular, the connection order of seven vertices cannot be determined from their positions alone. The historical source plates are reproduced here with permission from the estate of John Michell, while the explanatory diagrams are independent mathematical visualisations rather than facsimiles. Their distinct evidential roles are maintained throughout the analysis.

Our method separates (i) historical source statements; (ii) fixed generative reconstructions; (iii) calibrated measurement of printed artefacts; and (iv) independent mathematical consequences. Source coordinates are reserved for evaluating candidate geometry and do not silently alter the generator. The resulting claim is therefore source consistency under specified comparisons, not independent statistical validation of a design already informed by that source.

The central examples illuminate complementary limitations of visual evidence: the nonuniform twelve-circle arrangement is deduced from explicit incidences; the Figure~12 wall cannot be uniquely inferred across all residual metrics; Figure~14's stroke connectivity favours a $\{7/2\}$ over a $\{7/3\}$ heptagram; and the two later sevenfold approximation procedures must be considered in light of classical constructibility. A brief closing discussion of dimensional and Platonic-number interpretations shows why geometric agreement is not the same thing as historical transmission.

\subsection{Historical witnesses and source control}

The principal Michell source for the Figure~12 and Figure~14 plate analyses is
the 1973 Abacus edition of \emph{City of Revelation}
\citep{michell1973city}.  Michell's two approximate sevenfold procedures in
Figure~194, together with his later report of dimensions attributed to Ian
Sommerville, are audited from \emph{How the World Is Made}
\citep{michell2009world}.  Michell's numerical interpretation of Plato's eight
whorls is taken from the purchased 2008 digital edition of
\emph{The Dimensions of Paradise} \citep{michell2008dimensions}.

For Plato, the relevant \emph{Republic} passage is audited against frozen
English and Greek Perseus TEI witnesses for 616d--617b.  The underlying
English translation is Shorey's Loeb edition \citep{plato1935republic}; the
Greek text derives from Burnet's Oxford edition
\citep{plato1902republic}.  The project stores the two XML witnesses at a
fixed upstream Perseus revision and verifies them cryptographically
\citep{perseusRepublicWitnesses}.  Bibliographic identity and digital-witness
identity are therefore recorded separately.

Only Michell's two selected historical drawings reproduced in this article
are included under written permission from the estate; the complete source
book scan and purchased ebook screenshots are not redistributed with the
repository. Source identities and measurements remain anchored by local
manifests, page-level records and SHA-256 hashes.  The 2008 Wikimedia
diagram is treated only as an external computational reconstruction,
\texttt{NJG\_SVG}, rather than as a Michell primary source
\citep{anonmoos2008newjerusalem}.  The unresolved Sommerville 1974 primary
item remains \ArchivePending{} and is not assigned invented bibliographic
metadata.

\subsection{Normalized geometry and deterministic generation}

The geometric model is expressed in a normalized unit system.  The cardinal
core uses Earth radius $5.5$, Moon radius $1.5$, construction-circle radius
$7$, and Earth-square side $11$.  These dimensions preserve Michell's
Earth--Moon proportions while separating the construction from any later
choice of physical scale.

The complete \NJG{} composite adds the twelve-Moon system, the preferred
outer-wall project reconstruction, Michell's approximate Method~1 sevenfold
construction, the reciprocal fourteenfold layer, the four-triangle 28-point
scaffold, and the scaffold-derived Figure~14 candidate.  The generative
builder accepts only the overall geometric unit; it does not accept measured
source-plate coordinates or calibration parameters.

This distinction is architectural rather than merely procedural.  A candidate
geometry is first generated from registered construction rules.  Only
afterwards is it exposed to the historical plate-analysis pipeline.  The
source image can therefore evaluate a frozen construction but cannot silently
alter the geometry that it is being used to test.

\subsection{Source-plate calibration and no-refit comparison}

Printed diagrams are treated as measured historical artefacts rather than
ideal Euclidean drawings.  The Figure~12 and Figure~14 workflows therefore
separate image preparation, landmark or endpoint digitisation, coordinate
registration, candidate generation, and residual evaluation.

For Figure~12, the printed plate is affinely calibrated before frozen
candidate Moon placements and wall rules are compared.  The registered
diagnostics include directional, support-distance, and vertex-based residuals;
no single residual is assumed in advance to establish historical uniqueness.
The resulting metric dependence is retained explicitly in the interpretation.

For Figure~14, vertex location and edge topology are evaluated as distinct
evidence channels.  This separation is necessary because regular
$\{7/2\}$ and $\{7/3\}$ heptagrams use the same seven vertices while connecting
them differently.  Endpoint agreement alone therefore cannot determine the
printed star topology.  Once the candidate constructions and comparison
rules are frozen, source coordinates are not used to refit scale, rotation,
endpoint identity, or the underlying construction grammar except where a
registration model is explicitly part of the calibrated plate comparison.

\subsection{Exact constructibility analysis}

The exact sevenfold problem is analysed independently of plate fitting.
Writing
\begin{equation}
y=2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{7}\right),
\end{equation}
the target coordinate satisfies
\begin{equation}
y^3+y^2-2y-1=0.
\end{equation}
The polynomial is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, so the target has algebraic
degree three.  Ordinary straightedge-and-compass constructions generate
coordinates through successive quadratic extensions; an element of degree
three is therefore outside that closure.

The project supplements this theorem-level obstruction with a finite
construction-grammar test.  A frozen census of native Euclidean incidences is
searched only after exclusions are defined to prevent direct or disguised
injection of the target.  That census supplies a computational check within
the registered grammar; it is not the basis of the algebraic impossibility
result.

A separate extension then permits one explicitly cubic-capable operation,
\texttt{TRISECT\_ANGLE}.  The operation is applied to an angle constructed
from dimensions already native to the New Jerusalem geometry.  The resulting
triple-angle relation generates the same cubic satisfied by
$2\cos(2\pi/7)$.  Consequently, the later minimality statement is deliberately
restricted to the frozen operation-count model: zero cubic-capable operations
are excluded, and one such operation is sufficient.  It is not a claim that
angle trisection is uniquely simplest among all possible non-Euclidean
construction technologies.


\section{Core New Jerusalem reconstruction}

\subsection{Circles, square and the nonuniform twelve-centre arrangement}
The original published arrangement is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:michell12-original}. Our mathematically generated construction below distinguishes source incidence from measured plate coordinates.
\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.58\linewidth]{figures/licensed_historical/Michell1973_Figure12_ORIGINAL_CROP.pdf}
\caption{John Michell's historical Figure~12, ``The New Jerusalem'', reproduced from \emph{City of Revelation} (Abacus, 1973), p.~63. The original printed design is the artefact evaluated in the present source-calibrated analysis; it is not a project reconstruction. Reproduced by permission. \textcopyright{} The estate of John Michell.}
\label{fig:michell12-original}
\end{figure}

Let $O=(0,0)$ be the common centre, and measure lengths in units $u$ of 720 miles. In Michell's stated proportional scheme, the Earth radius is $3960$ miles and the Moon radius is $1080$ miles. The normalized quantities are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:basic}
 a=5.5\,u,\qquad r=1.5\,u,\qquad R=a+r=7\,u.
\end{equation}
The Earth circle has radius $a$. The square about it has side $2a=11\,u$ and boundaries $x=\pm a$ and $y=\pm a$. The construction circle of Moon centres has radius $R$. Each Moon circle has radius $r$ and its centre $M$ obeys $|OM|=R=a+r$, so it is externally tangent to the Earth circle. Four centres lie on the axes at $(\pm R,0)$ and $(0,\pm R)$.

The remaining eight centres are \emph{not} placed at equally spaced $30^\circ$ intervals. For the first-quadrant centre next to the positive horizontal axis, let the point $Q$ be where the right square edge meets the circle of centres:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q}
 Q=\left(a,\sqrt{R^2-a^2}\right),\qquad |OQ|=R.
\end{equation}
The historical incidence description requires the Moon circle about the oblique centre $M$ to pass through $Q$. Consequently, $|OM|=|OQ|=R$ and $|MQ|=r$. Define the direction of $OQ$ from the positive horizontal ray by $\psi$, and the angular separation of $OM$ and $OQ$ by $\gamma$. The isosceles triangle $OMQ$ then yields
\begin{align}
\psi&=\operatorname{atan2}\left(\sqrt{R^2-a^2},a\right)
       =38.213210701738^\circ,\\
\gamma&=2\arcsin\left(\frac{r}{2R}\right)
       =12.301279655882^\circ,\\
\beta&=\psi-\gamma
       =25.911931045856^\circ.\label{eq:beta}
\end{align}
Here $\beta$ is explicitly measured \emph{from the positive horizontal ray through $O$ to the ray $OM$}. It is \emph{not} the smaller angle $\angle QOM=\gamma$. By quarter-turns and reflections, the complete set of twelve centre directions is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:centres}
 \big\{k\cdot90^\circ,\quad k\cdot90^\circ-\beta,\quad k\cdot90^\circ+\beta\big\},\qquad k=0,1,2,3.
\end{equation}
Thus the geometry has four groups of three circles, quarter-turn and reflection symmetry, but not equal angular spacing. Figure~\ref{fig:twelve} gives both the full arrangement and an enlarged construction of $\beta$.
\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/pdf/jmaa_a02_twelve_circle_beta.pdf}
\caption{Independent analytic reconstruction of the twelve-circle arrangement (A) and an enlarged oblique-centre construction (B). The angle $\beta=\angle XOM$ is measured from the positive horizontal reference $OX$. The conditions $|OQ|=|OM|=7\,u$ and $|MQ|=1.5\,u$ determine its value. This is a mathematical explanatory figure, \emph{not} a facsimile of Michell's Figure~12.}\label{fig:twelve}
\end{figure}

Michell's square-perimeter and circle-circumference comparison uses the historical approximation $\pi=22/7$: the square perimeter is $4(11\,u)=44\,u$, and the circle of radius $7\,u$ has circumference $14\pi\,u$, only conventionally $44\,u$ under that approximation. The exact Euclidean incidence construction in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:q}--\eqref{eq:centres} must not be confused with that separate approximate identity. Moreover, $\beta$ is distinct from the $25.587895702680^\circ$ Moon-centre angle generated by the later approximate 28-point scaffold.

\section{Source-calibrated sevenfold geometry}

\subsection{Why identical vertices do not establish printed topology}
The printed visual artefact is reproduced in Figure~\ref{fig:michell14-original}; the independent comparator in Figure~\ref{fig:topology} will distinguish vertex positions from stroke connections.
\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.58\linewidth]{figures/licensed_historical/Michell1973_Figure14_ORIGINAL_CROP.pdf}

An ideal regular heptagram is described by its vertex set \emph{and} its edge traversal. A $\{7/2\}$ star connects every second point; a $\{7/3\}$ star connects every third. Both use precisely the same seven points, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:topology}. Therefore a small point-coordinate residual cannot, by itself, establish which star was printed.
\begin{figure}[htbp]\centering
\includegraphics[width=.94\linewidth]{figures/pdf/jmaa_a02_heptagram_topology.pdf}

The frozen Figure~14 audit made three repeated stroke-tracing passes near the registered endpoints and favoured $\{7/2\}$, superseding an earlier provisional $\{7/3\}$ interpretation. It identified five endpoints directly supported by the source's text/plate context; two positions in inter-Moon gaps remain project inferences. The conclusion is a \emph{near-regular $\{7/2\}$ topology}, not exact geometrical regularity of the printed artwork. In particular, the source-observed edge traversal and the modeled exact idealization are separate layers of evidence.



\subsection{Figure 14 topology}

project's earlier provisional $\{7/3\}$ interpretation.  The three frozen

\section{Exact sevenfold boundary}

\subsection{Classical Gauss--Wantzel obstruction and its relevance to Michell}
The inability to construct a regular heptagon with an unmarked straightedge and compass is an established result of classical mathematics, encompassed by the Gauss--Wantzel criterion; it is not first discovered in this paper \citep{wantzel1837,mathworldHeptagon}. Its relevance is specifically to the status of Michell's approximate sevenfold divisions. With
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cubic}
 y=2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{7}\right),\qquad y^3+y^2-2y-1=0,
\end{equation}
the displayed cubic is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ (it has no rational root $\pm 1$). Thus $[\mathbb{Q}(y):\mathbb{Q}]=3$. A number constructible from rational lengths by ordinary unmarked straightedge and compass has algebraic degree a power of two; hence the exact regular-sevenfold coordinate cannot be constructed by those means. This is a theorem-level obstruction, not a computational non-detection.

\subsection{Frozen native-incidence census}

The algebraic obstruction was supplemented by a finite computational census
within the project's registered native construction grammar.  After target
injection and equivalent disguised constructions were excluded in advance,
the frozen census contained 4873 semantic candidates and no exact sevenfold
solution.

The nearest remaining descriptive candidate had angular step
$51.4524438611^\circ$, differing from $360^\circ/7$ by approximately
$0.02387243^\circ$.  This finite result is not the proof of impossibility;
rather, it confirms that no exact sevenfold construction is hidden inside the
registered Euclidean grammar after the preregistered exclusions are applied.

\subsection{One-trisection cubic extension}

The preceding obstruction explains why a classical approximation cannot simply be upgraded to exact sevenfold geometry within the same rules. The following construction asks a different, explicitly counterfactual question: what happens when precisely one nonclassical cubic-capable operation is admitted? It is not a claim that Michell used trisection, nor a claim that general nonclassical heptagon constructions are novel.\par



After the ordinary Euclidean boundary was frozen, the project introduced one
explicitly cubic-capable operation, \texttt{TRISECT\_ANGLE}.  The construction
uses dimensions already native to the New Jerusalem geometry,
\[
R=7,\qquad D=3.
\]
From these,
\[
u=R-2D=1,\qquad
v=D\sqrt{3}=3\sqrt{3},\qquad
H=2\sqrt{7}.
\]
For the resulting anchor angle $\Theta$,
\[
\cos\Theta=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{7}}.
\]

One angle trisection defines
\[
\phi=\frac{\Theta}{3}.
\]
Setting
\[
z=\frac{2\sqrt{7}}{3}\cos\phi
\]
and applying the triple-angle identity gives
\[
z^3-\frac{7}{3}z-\frac{7}{27}=0.
\]
With
\[
y=z-\frac13,
\]
this becomes
\[
y^3+y^2-2y-1=0,
\]
the same irreducible cubic satisfied by
$2\cos(2\pi/7)$.

Within the frozen operation-count model, the result is therefore
\[
0\ \text{cubic-capable operations: impossible},
\qquad
1\ \text{cubic-capable operation: sufficient}.
\]
The minimality claim is deliberately restricted to that registered model.  It
does not establish that trisection is the unique or historically intended
non-Euclidean technology.

\section{Plato's eight whorls and Michell's numerical interpretation}

\subsection{Literal Plato layer}

The relevant passage of Plato's \emph{Republic} describes eight nested whorls
and gives the ordering of their rim widths.  Across the frozen English and
Greek witnesses, the width order is
\[
1>6>4>8>7>5>3>2
\]
\citep{plato1935republic,plato1902republic,perseusRepublicWitnesses}.

The audited passage supplies an ordinal ranking, not numerical magnitudes or
ratios for the widths.  Plato's separate ordering of rotational speeds is not
substituted for the rim-width order.  The project therefore classifies the
width ranking itself as \DirectPlato{} and does not infer a numerical sequence
from Plato alone.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{What the reconstruction establishes}

The principal result of this audit is not a single numerical match but a
separation of claims that are often visually conflated.  A substantial part
of Michell's New Jerusalem system can be represented as deterministic
Euclidean geometry: the normalized Earth--Moon core, the twelve-Moon
arrangement, the reciprocal fourteenfold continuation, the 28-point scaffold,
and associated sevenfold constructions can all be generated from explicit
rules without source-plate coordinates being used as hidden fitting
parameters.

The source-calibrated results have a different evidential status.  Figure~14
supports a near-regular $\{7/2\}$ topology because the printed stroke
connections, not merely the common seven-vertex set, distinguish it from the
alternative $\{7/3\}$ topology.  The Figure~12 outer wall is less uniquely
determined: the polar-pivot reconstruction gives the best registered
wall-direction agreement, whereas support distance alone favours another
candidate.  The appropriate conclusion is therefore source consistency under
specified metrics, not unique recovery of Michell's historical construction.

Michell's two Figure~194 procedures likewise emerge as distinct approximate
sevenfold constructions.  Method~1 is the more accurate of the two under the
registered local-angle comparison, while Method~2 admits the exact
project-derived identity
\[
\alpha_2=\arccos(5/8).
\]
Their errors lie on opposite sides of the exact $360^\circ/7$ step, but are
neither equal nor interpreted as a deliberate compensating pair.

The exact sevenfold result belongs to a third evidential category.  The
irreducible cubic for $2\cos(2\pi/7)$ establishes a theorem-level obstruction
to ordinary straightedge-and-compass construction independently of the
historical plates.  The subsequent one-trisection construction shows that a
single cubic-capable operation is sufficient within the frozen project
operation-count model.  That extension is a mathematical result of the
present work and is not attributed historically to Michell, Sommerville,
Plato, or an ancient source.

\subsection{Historical interpretation and evidential limits}

The metrological comparisons illustrate why numerical proximity alone is not
enough to establish historical significance.  The dodecagon radius classes
agree closely with values reported by Michell and attributed by him to Ian
Sommerville, but the underlying 1974 primary item has not been independently
frozen.  Moreover, the radius ratio is derived from the same two radius
classes and is therefore not an additional independent observation.  These
results support internal numerical consistency with Michell's later report,
not independent archival confirmation, statistical significance, or ancient
intentionality.

The Plato--Michell analysis imposes an analogous boundary.  Plato's
\emph{Republic} supplies an ordinal ordering of the eight rim widths.
Michell later supplies numerical values, a shaft rule, a centre-out
arrangement, and a scale factor.  Once those distinct source layers are kept
separate, Michell's numerical chain can be reconstructed exactly with zero
fitted parameters.  That exact reproduction verifies the internal arithmetic
of Michell's scheme; it does not move the numerical assignment backward into
Plato or demonstrate transmission from antiquity.

More generally, source plates used during model development cannot later be
treated as statistically independent validation of the same model.  The
present study therefore uses terms such as \emph{source-supported},
\emph{source-calibrated}, and \emph{source-consistent} deliberately.  They
describe a reproducible relation between a frozen construction and a historical
witness without claiming evidential independence that the workflow does not
possess.

\subsection{Limitations and reproducibility}

The explanatory figures are independently generated geometric drawings; they do not reproduce the line weights, drafting details or printed textures of Michell's original Figures~12 and~14 (1973, pp.~63 and~67). The calibrated historical comparisons are based on the identified source witnesses and remain distinct from these mathematical visualisations.\par



Several limitations remain.  Historical plate measurements are affected by
printing, scanning, line thickness, manual digitisation, and registration
uncertainty.  Some historical quantities entered model development and are
therefore unavailable as independent predictions.  The Sommerville 1974
primary source remains \ArchivePending{}.  The exact one-trisection result
establishes minimality only in the number of cubic-capable operations within
the registered construction grammar; it does not prove that angle trisection
is uniquely simplest among all possible extensions of Euclidean construction.

The study addresses Michell's planar New Jerusalem geometry and the specific
historical witnesses frozen for this audit.  It does not test a physical
mechanism, archaeological transmission hypothesis, or metaphysical
interpretation of the geometry.

Reproducibility is built into the analysis architecture.  The generative
geometry, source-calibration outputs, exact-sevenfold census, one-trisection
extension, historical source manifests, numerical reconstructions, and
publication figures are maintained as deterministic or hash-pinned artifacts
with automated tests.  The scientific content was frozen before manuscript
preparation, so editorial revision does not alter the numerical results or
provenance classifications reported here.

\section{Conclusion}

A provenance-aware computational audit resolves several distinct questions
within John Michell's New Jerusalem geometry without collapsing them into a
single claim of historical validation.  The principal geometric system can be
generated deterministically from explicit normalized rules.  Calibration
against Michell's printed figures supports a near-regular $\{7/2\}$ topology
for Figure~14, while the Figure~12 polar-pivot wall remains a preferred
source-supported reconstruction rather than a uniquely established historical
construction.

Michell's two approximate sevenfold procedures are quantitatively distinct:
Method~1 and Method~2 bracket the exact sevenfold angle, with Method~1 the
closer local approximation and Method~2 satisfying
$\alpha_2=\arccos(5/8)$.  Exact sevenfold geometry lies outside ordinary
straightedge-and-compass construction because its defining coordinate has
algebraic degree three.  Within the frozen project model, zero cubic-capable
operations are insufficient and one angle trisection is sufficient to recover
the exact cubic.

The historical analysis reaches a similarly bounded conclusion.  Michell's
reported dodecagon dimensions are closely reproduced but do not independently
verify the unresolved Sommerville source.  Plato supplies the ordinal
eight-whorl structure, Michell supplies the later numerical assignment, and
the present reconstruction verifies Michell's resulting arithmetic exactly
without attributing those numbers to Plato.

Taken together, these results show that Michell's New Jerusalem system contains
a substantial reproducible geometric and numerical structure while also
demonstrating where the surviving evidence no longer supports stronger
historical claims.  The same provenance-first approach can be applied to
future extensions without reopening results already frozen in the present
audit.
